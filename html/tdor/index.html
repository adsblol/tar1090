Trans Day of Remembrance20 November 2026

Once a year the trans community reads out the names of the people we lost to transphobic violence, and lights a candle for each of them. This page collects the commemorations that trans organisations around the world have announced for 20 November 2026 and the days around it.

Why today

Transgender Day of Remembrance began in 1999, after the murder of Rita Hester in Allston, Massachusetts. It is held every year on 20 November, at the close of Transgender Awareness Week. The ritual is simple and it is the same almost everywhere: names are read, candles are lit, and the number of the dead is spoken out loud.

The Dutch organisation Transgender Netwerk reports that 273 trans people were murdered worldwide in the past year, one murder every 32 hours, and that the count is always too low: many killings are never reported, or never recorded as anti-trans violence. Transgender Netwerk cites the Trans Murder Monitoring project. Bundesverband Trans* publishes the 2026 edition of the same monitoring together with its statement.

If you go to one of these gatherings: bring a candle, dress for the weather, and stay for the names. If there is nothing near you, light one at home at 18:00 your time.
